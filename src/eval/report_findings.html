• The headline accuracy depends on how the sentence is scored, because the corruptions change the sentence length. For Turkish (regular, epoch 5) the three accuracies are 85.50 / 90.77 / 85.53 on dat→acc and 84.90 / 88.20 / 84.83 on acc→dat (mean-NLL / total log-prob / edit-onward per-char surprisal). In 41% of all items the corrupted edit is longer than the gold edit (im → in den). On those 2,992 dat→acc items the original metric gives 74.4% and total log-prob 96.0%; on the 3,007 items where the corruption is shorter it is 94.1% vs 89.1%. Where the lengths are equal (3,390 items) the two metrics are identical (87.6%). See the length lens.
  • Gold-length normalisation is not length-neutral. Dividing both totals by the gold length compares total log-probabilities, so a longer corrupted sentence is penalised automatically and a shorter one favoured. Use the same-length subset (3,390 + 4,096 items, selectable everywhere below as “Same length”) when the question is about case rather than length. The edit-onward metric (acc_span: per-char surprisal from the first edited word to the end) is length-normalised like the mean-NLL metric and tracks it closely (within 0.1pp at epoch 5 for Turkish), so it does not remove the length effect.
  • German-only is ahead of every L1 system, under every metric; the gap depends on the metric. At epoch 5 (regular) German-only reaches 91.99 / 92.10% (dat→acc / acc→dat, mean-NLL). Turkish is behind it by −6.49 / −4.25 / −6.47pp on dat→acc and −7.20 / −5.18 / −7.33pp on acc→dat (mean-NLL / total log-prob / edit-onward; all q<0.001), and all 7 regular L1 systems are behind with q<0.001 under every metric (smallest gap: −4.5pp). On same-length items the Turkish deficit is −6.07pp (dat→acc) and −5.99pp (acc→dat), so it is not a length artefact. The deficit shrinks with German training (acc→dat total log-prob: −7.98pp at epoch 1 → −5.18pp at epoch 5). Caveat: German-only is not an “L1-free” control. Its phase 1 is 10M German sentences, so it sees German in both phases, and it uses the newer tokenizer (see the method notes). The next finding uses the matched German-only controls for reset and shuffled.
  • Against a matched German-only control, the reset gap disappears and the shuffled gap is small. German-only gets the same treatments as the L1 systems (embeddings reset before phase 2; word-shuffled German as phase 1). With reset embeddings it falls to 85.18 / 83.84% (dat→acc / acc→dat, epoch 5), −6.81 / −8.27pp below its regular run (q<0.001), i.e. to the level of the regular L1 systems. L1 reset systems are indistinguishable from it on dat→acc (EN −0.49, TR −0.10, ZH −0.25pp; no system has q<0.05 under any metric); on acc→dat they are at most 1.07pp below it (ZH), and only 4 of 7 reach q<0.05 on the original metric and 1 of 7 under total log-prob. With a shuffled German phase 1, German-only scores 86.92 / 86.02% (−5.07 / −6.09pp vs. regular, q<0.001). Shuffled L1 systems remain behind it: dat→acc EN −1.47, TR −1.40, ZH −2.63pp; acc→dat EN −1.70, TR −1.38, ZH −3.39pp (all 7 L1 systems, all three metrics, q<0.01). Epoch-5 values, mean-NLL; the control gap is smaller under total log-prob (e.g. shuffled dat→acc −0.48 / −0.72 / −1.15pp).
  • English and Turkish remain close; a small acc→dat edge for English depends on length normalisation. On dat→acc, EN − TR is +0.16 / +0.10 / +0.23pp (q between 0.06 and 0.38). On acc→dat the original metric gives +0.31pp (q=0.017) and edit-onward +0.35pp (q=0.040), but it falls to +0.06pp (q=0.66) under total log-prob and +0.12pp (q=0.62) on same-length items. Chinese, in contrast, is clearly behind both on acc→dat (EN − ZH and TR − ZH, q<0.02 under all three metrics).
  • Stripping Turkish morphology: same direction, about half the size. On acc→dat Turkish is ahead of the stripped variants in 12 of 12 cells (4 variants × 3 configs) under the original and total metrics and in 11 of 12 under edit-onward. After Benjamini–Hochberg correction within those 12 tests, 5 survive under the original metric (regular: Postp +0.58, Nominal Infl +0.57, Nominal +0.55pp; shuffled: Postp +0.50, Nominal Infl +0.51pp), 3 under total log-prob (regular: Postp +0.24, Nominal Infl +0.45, Nominal +0.28pp) and 4 under edit-onward (regular: Postp +0.48, Nominal +0.48; shuffled: Postp +0.49, Nominal Infl +0.50pp). On dat→acc none survives under any of the three (smallest q=0.23). The stripping cost is real in direction but small.
  • The word-order and embedding-reset costs also shrink under total log-prob. Shuffling the English L1 cost +0.89pp on acc→dat with the original metric (q<0.001) but +0.35pp (q=0.089) under total log-prob (edit-onward: +0.94pp, q<0.001). Resetting English embeddings cost +0.96pp on dat→acc (q=0.001) and still costs +0.42pp (q=0.034) under total log-prob. The Turkish reset cost on acc→dat is robust (+1.36 → +0.89pp, q≤0.003 for both).
  • Core arguments: far below the prepositional items, and far below German-only. Core items are split by UD part of speech of the NP head (PRON vs. other). On dat→acc (571 items, regular, epoch 5, mean-NLL) Turkish scores 53.3% on pronouns (German-only 79.5%, −26.2pp, q<0.001; this includes demonstrative and relative pronouns such as dem, denen) and 35.6% on other core arguments (German-only 71.0%, −35.4pp, q<0.001). Across all 21 L1 systems the “other” subset stays at 32–38%, below chance. Pronouns are 42% of the core items and account for 35% of the Turkish deficit on the core aggregate. On acc→dat (3,821 core items, mostly direct objects) the same split gives 87.4% vs 93.6% on pronouns (−6.3pp, q<0.001) and 89.8% vs 95.9% on other (−6.1pp, q<0.001). For comparison, on dat→acc dative-only prepositions are at 95.2% (German-only 97.4%) and two-way prepositions at 82.7% (89.8%); on acc→dat, accusative-only prepositions are at 95.7% (97.5%) and two-way prepositions at 66.2% (80.8%).
  • The core-pronoun deficit does not depend on the L1. Relative to German-only (dat→acc, core pronouns): EN −25.7, TR −26.2, ZH −29.3, stripped variants −26.6 to −27.1pp. EN − TR is +0.5pp (q=0.74), and English and Turkish pronoun accuracy stays at 52–56% under reset and shuffled L1. The pronoun / non-pronoun split of all items (UD PRON head; 7.5% of the items in each test) shows a deficit for both groups in both directions (Turkish vs. German-only, original metric, epoch 5: −11.6 / −6.1pp on dat→acc, −6.6 / −7.3pp on acc→dat; descriptive, not tested).
  • Edited-span length matters, but differently per direction. Edit-onward accuracy for Turkish on dat→acc is lowest for the shortest edits and flat beyond (1–3 chars 78.9%, 4–6 91.7%, 7–10 92.1%, 11–20 92.8%, 21+ 92.4%), whereas on acc→dat it varies without a trend (84.0, 88.0, 81.9, 81.7, 87.1%). Short spans are typically single function-word changes (im → in den, mir → mich), so only a few characters separate the two forms. Use the span-length section for other metrics and systems.
